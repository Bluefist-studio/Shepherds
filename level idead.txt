1. First Gate
Concept: Simple introduction to gate movement.

Small starting enclosure
One narrow gate
Open field after the gate
Pasture directly beyond it
Mostly green sheep
One orange separator
Tests:

Dog positioning
Gate following
Keeping the flock together
Basic pressure control
2. Split Flock
Concept: Start with two separate flocks that must be merged.

Two flock spawn points
One pasture
No fences in the starting area
A few rocks between the groups
Mostly green and yellow sheep
Tests:

Multiple flock starts
Flock merging
Movement alignment
Nervousness control
3. Nervous Gatekeeper
Concept: An orange or red separator is placed near the flock.

One orange sheep
Several green sheep
Small enclosed pasture
One narrow gate
The dog must avoid making the separator too nervous too early
Tests:

Separator behavior
Nervousness spreading
Green sheep following a nervous separator
Preventing a small subgroup from breaking away

4. Mother and Lamb Yard
Concept: Move blue mothers and light-blue lambs through a controlled route.

Two blue mothers
Two light-blue lambs per mother
A few green sheep
One large enclosure
One gate on the far side
Rocks near the edges
Tests:

Permanent mother/lamb links
Lamb tether strength
Mother movement
Family nervousness sharing
5. Narrow Corridor
Concept: Move the flock through a long narrow passage.

Two long parallel fences
One opening at the far end
Pasture beyond the corridor
No large open escape areas
Mostly green sheep with two yellow sheep
Tests:

Dog pressure positioning
Edge buffering
Sheep collision behavior
Preventing sheep from getting stuck or turning around

6. Corner Pasture
Concept: Pasture occupies a corner, forcing the player to approach from the correct side.

Large open field
Pasture in the top-right corner
Fence along two pasture sides
Gate near one corner
Rocks blocking the direct center route
Tests:

Gate direction
Dog positioning around corners
Sheep obstacle steering
Holding the flock in the pasture

7. Three Small Pens
Concept: Several small areas connected by gates.

Starting pen on the left
Middle pen with two rocks
Pasture pen on the right
One gate between each pen
Gates placed at opposite corners
Tests:

Gate propagation
Flock following the first sheep
Moving through multiple transitions
Managing sheep that stop at gate entrances

8. Separator Escape
Concept: One red sheep must be managed without losing the main flock.

One red sheep
Several green sheep
One blue mother and one lamb
Open field
Pasture on the opposite side
No fences except the pasture
Tests:

Red separator breakaway
Nervousness spread
Greens being pulled into a subgroup
Mother/lamb cohesion under pressure

9. Obstacle Maze
Concept: Use rocks to create several possible routes.

Large open field
8–12 rock obstacles
Pasture behind the obstacle field
No fences before the pasture
One gate into the pasture
Tests:

Obstacle steering
Flock movement alignment
Preventing sheep from splitting around obstacles
Choosing where to apply pressure

10. Calm Before the Storm
Concept: A large calm flock with a few sensitive sheep.

30 green sheep
5 yellow sheep
2 orange sheep
1 red sheep
2 blue mothers
2 light-blue lambs
Large open field
Large pasture with a wide gate
Tests:

Mixed flock behavior
Nervousness spreading
Family grouping
Separator behavior
Broad flock movement

11. Timed Sprint
Concept: A compact level designed around speed.

Small open field
Short distance to pasture
One gate
Mostly green sheep
Timer set relatively low
Tests:

Fast dog movement
Efficient pressure
Star rating
Whether the timer feels fair

12. Recovery Field
Concept: Sheep start close to the pasture but in a difficult arrangement.

Flock starts in three small groups
Pasture nearby
One red sheep in an outer group
One blue mother separated from its lamb
Several rocks between groups
Tests:

Multiple flock starts
Family recovery
Reuniting groups
Short-distance herding
Strong First Five
